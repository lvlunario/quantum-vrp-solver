## 09:10–10:30 — Derive the aggregation contract

For state probabilities `p_s`, I defined physical mass as the sum of `p_s`
over physically feasible states, auxiliary mass over encoding-consistent
states, and joint mass over states where both verdicts pass. The four category
masses must partition one.

The important engineering decision is that the aggregator receives the
probability vector as an explicit argument. It does not interpret “64 rows” as
uniform. Support indices must be complete, unique and ordered; source verdicts
must be Booleans; each category must agree with those verdicts; weights must be
finite, nonnegative and normalized within a fixed absolute tolerance.

An early validation draft checked only the total weight. That would have
allowed a duplicated state and omitted another state while still summing to
one. I tightened the interface to require index `i` in position `i`, then
added a duplicate-index negative control.

## 10:45–12:20 — Execute four synthetic distributions

The audit reused the fixed 64-state capacity fixture and constructed four
distributions. Uniform weights reproduced physical mass `0.625` and auxiliary
and joint masses `0.078125`. A point mass on a fully valid state returned one
for all three measures. A point mass on a physically feasible state with wrong
slack returned physical mass one but auxiliary and joint mass zero.

The final three-point mixture assigned 0.25 to a fully valid state, 0.50 to a
physical-only state and 0.25 to an infeasible state. Its masses were 0.75,
0.25 and 0.25 respectively. These exact constructed cases test the separation
more clearly than a random distribution would.

Eight checks passed. Seven malformed cases were rejected: mismatched length,
negative weight, NaN, Boolean weight, a total of one-half, duplicate support
index and a category tampered to disagree with its source verdicts. Category
masses summed to one and every reported mass stayed within probability bounds.
