## 10:40–11:25 — Derive P01 without running it

I pre-registered nonnegative-demand monotonicity. For fixed demand vector and
capacity, if binary decision `x` is componentwise no greater than `y`, then
turning on the extra loads cannot reduce total load or overload. If the larger
decision `y` remains feasible, the smaller `x` must also be feasible.

The direction matters. An infeasible larger decision says nothing automatic
about whether the smaller one is feasible, and a feasible smaller decision
does not guarantee the larger one is feasible. Auxiliary consistency is also
excluded: changing a decision while holding slack bits fixed can make an
equality either better or worse.

For three decision bits, each ordered component has three possibilities:
`(0,0)`, `(0,1)`, or `(1,1)`. The complete bounded test therefore contains
`3^3 = 27` ordered pairs, including eight identity pairs. This count and the
six planned checks were frozen before any pair was evaluated.
