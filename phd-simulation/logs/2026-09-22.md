## 09:45–11:30 — Defining the toy
You define three loads requiring 2, 3 and 4 units, with service priorities 3, 5 and 6. Available capacity is 5. A binary one means the load is served; the cost is the total priority left unserved. The optimal feasible assignment serves the first two loads, uses five capacity units and leaves cost 6.

The tempting shortcut is to remove the hard constraint and add a penalty for exceeding capacity. With penalty weight 0.1, serving everything gives an objective of only 1.6, despite overloading the system by four units. An optimizer that chooses this solution may be doing exactly what its programmer asked. That is a requirements-to-formulation failure.

## 12:00–14:00 — EXECUTED LOCAL EXPERIMENT
A small Python/NumPy implementation prepares a uniform three-qubit state, applies a diagonal cost phase and a depth-one X mixer. It searches 1,681 angle pairs for each of three penalty settings. These are ideal statevector calculations on synthetic data, with no quantum hardware or random measurement shots.

| Penalty | Penalized exact minimizer feasible? | QAOA probability of feasible decision | QAOA probability of optimal feasible decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.1 | No | 1.49% | 0.44% |
| 1.0 | Yes | 74.25% | 43.12% |
| 4.0 | Yes | 76.60% | 60.35% |

For reference, uniform sampling is feasible 62.5% of the time and selects the optimal feasible assignment 12.5% of the time. This comparison is descriptive, not a fair end-to-end speed benchmark: the QAOA parameters were searched using thousands of exact evaluations. Exhaustive enumeration needs only eight assignments here.

Raw values, environment and source hash are in [the recorded output](../results/day001-toy.json). [The code](../src/toy_qaoa.py) is reproducible. Three checks passed: the manually enumerated feasible cost set, normalization/zero-phase behavior, and an independent one-qubit analytic probability formula.

## 14:00–15:00 — What did not work yet
The piecewise penalty has been encoded directly as a diagonal oracle. Calling this a finished QUBO implementation would be wrong. A slack-variable encoding and circuit decomposition are future work. More importantly, this small example is not a novel result; penalty mistakes are an obvious concern. The prospective contribution must be a useful, generalizable verification method supported by prior-work analysis.

## 15:00–16:30 — Writing and next decision
You write the initial manuscript's problem statement, methods and limitations, then outline a short lab talk. The day's strongest result is a clearer boundary around what the evidence supports. You finish with a next task: derive a sufficient penalty bound and verify it against every assignment before moving to a larger model.

**In plain language:** a solver can be excellent at the mathematical problem you gave it and still give an unusable engineering answer. Testing must check both.
