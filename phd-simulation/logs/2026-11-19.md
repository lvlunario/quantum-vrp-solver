## 09:05–10:10 — Derive the two smallest witnesses

I fixed `x=(0,0,0)` and `y=(1,0,0)`. Both decisions are physically feasible:
their loads are zero and two under capacity five.

With slack five, `x` exactly satisfies the equality while `y` overshoots it by
two. Auxiliary consistency changes from true to false. With slack three, `x`
is two below the equality and `y` reaches it exactly, so consistency changes
from false to true.

These two witnesses use the same ordered decision pair and change only the
fixed slack assignment. Together they show that auxiliary consistency is
neither upward-preserving nor downward-preserving in the decision ordering.
They do not contradict physical feasibility because both decisions remain
safe in both cases.

## 12:45–14:05 — Repair an interpretation mistake

My first notes called a true→false transition “worsening encoding quality.”
That phrasing was too strong. The Boolean flag says whether one exact equality
holds; it does not measure distance from consistency. A residual moving from
minus four to minus two is closer to zero while the flag remains false, and a
residual can cross zero and become false again.

I revised the documentation to call the result nonmonotonicity of the equality
indicator, not degradation. A future graded diagnostic could use absolute
residual, but that would be a different metric requiring its own contract and
tests.
