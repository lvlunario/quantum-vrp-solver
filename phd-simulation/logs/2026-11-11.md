## 09:05–10:30 — Build the two-report adapter

I implemented `evaluate_state`, which first calls the independent physical
oracle on the three load decisions. It then validates the three auxiliary bits
and positive integer weights before calculating slack and the equality
residual `load + slack - capacity`.

The output contains `physical.feasible` and `auxiliary.consistent` as separate
fields. A descriptive category can say both valid, physical only, auxiliary
only or neither, but downstream analysis must retain the two source verdicts.
The distinction prevents a representation defect from being described as an
unsafe load decision.

The first draft of the analysis tempted me to call the two uniform fractions
“probabilities.” I changed that wording before publication. Today enumerates a
uniform set of 64 bitstrings; it does not sample a circuit. The retained result
therefore calls them counting masses and records zero statevectors, searches,
shots and hardware jobs.

## 10:45–12:10 — Execute the 64-state partition

The verifier enumerated every combination of three decision bits and three
slack bits. Forty states contain a physically feasible load decision because
five of eight decisions are feasible and each appears with eight slack
assignments. Only five states have a consistent slack assignment: exactly one
for each feasible decision.

The resulting partition is five states where both reports pass, 35 where only
physical feasibility passes, zero auxiliary-only states, and 24 where neither
passes. Eight checks passed, including exact enumeration, the hand-derived
partition, the `40/64` and `5/64` counts, subset behavior, one-consistent-slack
counts, residual identities and five rejected malformed auxiliary definitions.

The zero auxiliary-only count is a property of this formulation, not a general
law. With nonnegative slack, equality `load + slack = capacity` cannot hold for
an already overloaded decision. A different representation would require a
new argument and new tests.
