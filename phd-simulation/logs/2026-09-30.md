## 09:15–11:00 — Coursework becomes an interface specification
The derivation starts from a binary value b and defines s=1-2b. Zero becomes plus one; one becomes minus one. Substituting b=(1-s)/2 into each linear and pairwise term produces an Ising expression. Pair terms contribute to the constant and to two local fields, as well as to a spin-pair coefficient. That bookkeeping is exactly where a plausible-looking implementation can go wrong.

The resulting [mapping note](../docs/ising-mapping.md) gives the general formulas and the coefficients for this instance. It also specifies the logical variable order. This feels more like writing an interface-control document than inventing a new algorithm, which is appropriate: the research question concerns trustworthy transitions between representations. A correct optimizer cannot repair a wrong translation of the problem it receives.
