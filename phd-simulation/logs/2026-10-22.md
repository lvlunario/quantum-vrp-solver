## 09:10–11:00 — Read Quito at the oracle boundary

I upgraded Quito from metadata-level screening to direct reading of the
original ICST study and the later ASE tool paper. Quito treats a program as
valid classical inputs mapped to probability distributions over measured
classical outputs. Correctness assessment requires a user-supplied program
specification.

The Wrong Output Oracle has a crisp condition: an observed output assigned
probability zero by that specification is a definite failure. The Output
Probability Oracle is statistical. It compares repeated observed proportions
with specified probabilities using a one-sample Wilcoxon signed-rank test.
Rejecting the null supports a likely failure at the selected significance
level; failing to reject is explicitly inconclusive, not a pass.

That wording changes how this project should report sampled tests. More shots
cannot turn absent evidence into proof of correctness. Budgets, grouping of
repeated suites and significance choices all affect conclusions. The original
study also says its black-box coverage does not cover phase, used five small
programs, seeded one mutation at a time, and evaluated ideal simulator qubits
without modeled hardware error. Quito is therefore relevant to future sampled
readout testing but cannot replace our exact pre-mixer phase oracle.

## 11:00–12:15 — Read QDiff without borrowing its certainty

QDiff addresses quantum software stacks rather than a single application
formula. Its generator applies gate rewrites intended to preserve the unitary,
creating syntactically different variants that should behave alike. After
execution, it averages the observed distributions of a variant group and uses
that average as a reference. Individual results are compared with
Kolmogorov–Smirnov or cross-entropy distances using a threshold and an estimated
measurement budget.

The important limitation is structural: the reference is collective, not an
independently derived expected answer. Divergence is valuable evidence of
inconsistency, and varying frontend versus backend options can suggest a
potential source, but agreement can still be common-mode wrong. Measurement
estimates also depend on a confidence target and platform-calibrated constant.
For the current ideal six-qubit fixture, the independent exact statevector
oracle is stronger on mathematical correctness. QDiff becomes useful later when
testing SDK, compiler and backend interactions that the local oracle does not
exercise.
